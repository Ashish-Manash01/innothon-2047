Mix model

A least-cost optimiser decides who generates what, hour by season

Sees night and monsoon

12 time slices: 4 seasons × day, evening, night. Solar is absent at night and dims in the monsoon; wind peaks June–September. An annual-only version gave 0% coal, so we rejected it.

Respects real limits

NISE solar, NIWE wind and CEA hydro potential; build speed (solar 1,279 GW, wind 375 GW by 2047); 229 GW of committed coal; nuclear ≤ 100 GW; corridors 3× today.

Keeps the lights on

15% reserve over peak; coal never below 40% of its evening output; 4-hour batteries and 8-hour pumped storage cycle daily with losses; CO₂ ≤ 1.2 Gt.

Linear programme solved with HiGHS in under a second per scenario, so we ran 135 policy combinations for the dashboard. Costs are 2026 tariffs: solar ₹2.5–3.0, wind ₹3–4, coal ₹5–6.3, nuclear ₹6–7 per kWh.

The optimiser is the honest way to answer where each region's power should come from: it has to meet demand in every season and time of day at the lowest cost, within physical and policy limits. The time slices matter. Without them, solar looked able to do everything.
