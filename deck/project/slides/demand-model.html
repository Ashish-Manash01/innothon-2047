Demand model

Electricity generated per person rises 0.76% for every 1% rise in income

Fitted on generation only

National generation per person vs real GDP per person, FY11–FY26, growth on growth with COVID terms: elasticity 0.761 (SE 0.104). A levels fit on the post-shortage years gives 0.837. No consumption or capacity data.

Tested on what happened

Fitted to FY19 only, the model predicts the next years well: FY24: +0.7%, FY25: +1.0%, FY26: +6.2%. A naive trend misses by more (FY24: +4.8%, FY25: +5.4%, FY26: +10.4%). FY26 was a cool, wet year.

Regions start from where power is needed

Each region's requirement = generation inside it + net imports from other regions (NPP/CEA reports, Grid-India flows), averaged FY24–26. Then it grows with its own GSDP and population.

Projection: UN population (1,665 million in 2047), GDP at 6.5% a year, regional GSDP and population from the official INNOTHON dataset and MoSPI, elasticity easing to 75% of today's by 2047 as demand matures. Urbanisation was tested and adds nothing beyond income, so it works through income.

Why this model: the rules say generation is the only basis, so we fitted the response of generation to income directly. It explains itself, and fitted on data only up to 2019 it predicted 2024 and 2025 within about one percent.
